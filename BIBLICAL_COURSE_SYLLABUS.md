# Lion's Gate — Biblical Course Syllabus

Established October 27, 2026; publication/lesson update October 28. The original educational design is preserved. All 50 survey records are now live under explicitly authorized AI-assisted review; Scripture lesson labels are implemented, while the future historical selector and journal remain proposals. This historical course **adds a second organizational axis** to the 18 subjects in [BIBLICAL_CURRICULUM_AND_DOCTRINE.md](BIBLICAL_CURRICULUM_AND_DOCTRINE.md); it does not replace them or change the current selector, ranks, armor, saves or gameplay. Successful completion of Level 700 remains the planned Kingdom-entry milestone, not an implemented transformation.

The goal is understanding the whole biblical narrative and every identifiable Torah commandment. Fifty survey questions cannot accomplish that goal. All 187 Torah chapter slots still require a comprehensive commandment/objective catalog, including recipients, stated purposes, conditions, exceptions and applications. No chapter or numerical total of commandments is certified by this syllabus.

## Source and teaching rules

Use the project's traditional 80-book KJV collection, including the traditional 14 Apocrypha books. Scripture governs the answer, not personal research, later religious custom or an unstated interpretation. Use biblical peoples and genealogies without modern racial classification. Marriage and inheritance restrictions must retain their passage-specific recipients and conditions. Do not label a command obsolete merely because its original setting was ancient Israel. Distinguish prescribed conduct, narrative description, prophetic promise and a proposed application.

Read Yeshua and the Apostles in their Torah and prophetic contexts. Teach the enduring authority of Yah's word, obedience, mercy and righteous judgment together. Explain prophetic national accountability and the Messiah's authority without turning divine judgment into permission for private violence. Do not force a chronology, modern identification or denominational conclusion that a passage does not state. KJV wording is retained in evidence; explanations may use Yah and Yeshua without altering quotations.

## Four recurring outcome levels

Foundational outcomes identify an explicit action and its stated meaning, not just a name or number. Intermediate outcomes follow context, cause, conditions and consequences. Advanced outcomes compare passages and distinguish their respective claims. Master outcomes apply interacting legal or leadership conditions, evaluate competing explanations and state the limits of the evidence. These are learning goals, not newly implemented gameplay stages.

Editorial ratings 1–10 map through `editorial-1-10-v1`: ratings 1–2→complexity 1; 3→2; 4–5→3; 6→4; 7→5; 8–9→6; 10→7. Runtime display remains 1 for ratings 1–3, 2 for 4–6, 3 for 7–10. Neither several citations nor long wording automatically deserves a high rating. Reader calibration remains pending.

## Unit 01 — Creation, the Fall, and the Beginning

- **Objectives:** Explain image-bearing, humanity's commission, sanctified rest, disobedience and restricted access to life. Distinguish explicit explanation from speculation about origins or inherited guilt.
- **Primary passages / events:** Genesis 1–5: creation, seventh-day rest, Eden, expulsion, Cain and Abel, early generations.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Obedience to the given command; responsibility for life and conduct; rest (01, 02, 06; general foundations). Marriage's one-flesh teaching connects to subject 07 without adding unstated restrictions.
- **Prophetic connections:** Study Isaiah 65:17–25 and the new-creation imagery alongside Genesis; distinguish the respective scenes before harmonizing them.
- **NT cross-references:** Romans 5:12–21; Matthew 19:4–6; Revelation 22:1–5, 14–15.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—explain why the day is sanctified. Intermediate—trace disobedience and expulsion. Advanced—compare Adam and Christ as Paul does. Master—evaluate a claim about life, judgment and restoration across Genesis, Romans and Revelation while identifying what the texts leave unresolved.
- **Initial questions:** Five; creation purpose, rest rationale, moral responsibility, expulsion and Adam–Christ comparison. Further work: generations, human relationships and careful treatment of Genesis 3:15 without speculative detail.

## Unit 02 — Noah, Babel, and the Nations

- **Objectives:** Explain flood judgment, preservation, covenant assurance, blood/life obligations and Babel's reversal; distinguish genealogy from modern identity claims.
- **Primary passages / events:** Genesis 6–11: violence, ark, flood, altar, bow, nations and Babel.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Human life, blood restrictions, fruitfulness, responsibility before the Creator (01, 04, 13, 18; general foundations).
- **Prophetic connections:** Isaiah 54:9–10 uses Noah's waters in an assurance; study the comparison's scope rather than assuming every covenant has identical terms.
- **NT cross-references:** Matthew 24:37–39; Hebrews 11:7; 1 Peter 3:18–22; Acts 17:24–31. Mark disputed applications for separate review.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—identify the stated cause of judgment. Intermediate—trace Babel's intended and actual outcomes. Advanced—distinguish covenant sign, recipients, promise and obligation. Master—compare the nations narrative with apostolic accountability without extrapolating unprovided identities or dates.
- **Initial questions:** Five; violence, post-flood promise/duty, image-grounded life accountability, scattering and nations seeking God. Further work: bounded comparisons of flood and later judgment.

## Unit 03 — Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and the Covenant

- **Objectives:** Trace the call, promises, sign, household responsibility and inheritance expectation across the patriarchs.
- **Primary passages / events:** Genesis 12–36: departure, covenant, Isaac's birth and testing, covenant continuation, Jacob's journeys and return.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Obeying Yah's voice; the Genesis 17 sign and its named recipients; justice in the household; land and family obligations (01, 07, 09, 16, 17).
- **Prophetic connections:** Micah 7:18–20; Isaiah 51:1–3. Study named promises and mercy before proposing fulfillment structures.
- **NT cross-references:** Acts 7:2–8; Romans 4; Galatians 3:6–18; Hebrews 11:8–19. Preserve each writer's argument instead of inferring that promise removes obedience.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—identify promise and response. Intermediate—distinguish household sign recipients and justice duties. Advanced—trace continuity through Isaac and Jacob. Master—reconcile sojourning, limited present possession and continuing expectation without calling the promise revoked or inventing a fulfillment timetable.
- **Initial questions:** Five; promise continuity, delayed return, sign scope, household justice and inheritance expectation. Further work: covenant ratification, intercession and passage-bounded marriage/lineage studies.

## Unit 04 — The Twelve Tribes, Joseph, and Egyptian Captivity

- **Objectives:** Follow Jacob's household, Joseph's suffering and administration, tribal inheritance and the transition from refuge to bondage.
- **Primary passages / events:** Genesis 29–30; 37–50; Exodus 1–2: sons, dreams, sale, famine, migration, blessings, Joseph's death and oppression.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Family responsibility, preserving life, resource stewardship, righteous leadership and inheritance (07–11, 16, 17).
- **Prophetic connections:** Genesis 49 supplies tribal blessing language; Ezekiel 37:15–28 later names Judah and Joseph. Do not use these as a modern ancestry chart.
- **NT cross-references:** Acts 7:9–19; Hebrews 11:21–22. Compare the particular point of each retelling; unresolved numerical or burial-detail questions need separate review.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—explain preparation for famine. Intermediate—follow envy, sale and reconciliation; distinguish Ephraim and Manasseh's position. Advanced—relate the promise of departure to later oppression. Master—distinguish human evil intent, divine preservation and a leader's duty without justifying wrongdoing by its results.
- **Initial questions:** Five; Judah's responsibility for Benjamin, famine policy, inheritance adoption, oppression and promise, providence versus moral excuse. Further work: all twelve tribes, migration and blessings.

## Unit 05 — Moses, the Exodus, Sinai, and the Giving of the Law

- **Objectives:** Explain redemption as covenant relationship and inheritance; trace Passover, deliverance, wilderness provision and Sinai's obligations and ratification.
- **Primary passages / events:** Exodus 2–24; Deuteronomy 5–6: commission, plagues, Passover, sea crossing, manna, Sinai and covenant blood.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Passover instruction and memorial, Sabbath preparation, hearing and obeying, teaching children, covenant words (01–03, 06, 08, 12).
- **Prophetic connections:** Jeremiah 11:1–8 recalls the Egypt covenant; Jeremiah 31:31–34 names the broken covenant and promised new covenant.
- **NT cross-references:** Luke 22:14–20; 1 Corinthians 5:7–8; 10:1–13; Hebrews 9:18–22. Identify comparisons without erasing the original event and recipients.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—explain deliverance's purpose. Intermediate—connect memorial, provision and obedience. Advanced—compare Sinai/Horeb retellings with Hebrews. Master—distinguish mediator, assent, blood, promise and obligations across covenant accounts without pretending their detail lists are identical.
- **Initial questions:** Five; people and inheritance, Passover teaching, manna/rest, covenant condition and ratification comparison. Further work: crossing, testing, golden calf, mediation and covenant renewal.

## Unit 06 — The Torah, Commandments, Holiness, and Covenant Responsibilities

- **Objectives:** Systematically catalog Torah obligations, reasons, recipients and exceptions; apply bounded cases rather than generic religious assumptions.
- **Primary passages / events:** Exodus 20–40; Leviticus; Numbers; Deuteronomy. Begin with Leviticus 5, 10–11, 19–20, 23; Numbers 35; Deuteronomy 16–19, then audit every Torah chapter.
- **Obligations / subject links:** **All 18 subjects:** covenant, Sabbath, appointed times, diet, appearance, worship, marriage, vulnerable people, property, economics, land, priesthood, judges, purity, idolatry, leadership, restoration and judgment. Retain fringes/beards/modesty, prayer, menstrual/bodily purity, vows, inheritance and marriage as distinct objectives. Do not flatten recipient-specific judicial, priestly or sanctuary duties into an identical procedure for every person today.
- **Prophetic connections:** Ezekiel 44:15–31; Isaiah 56:1–8; Jeremiah 34; Malachi 2:1–9; land-rest warnings in Leviticus 26 alongside Chronicles.
- **NT cross-references:** Matthew 5:17–20; 15:1–20; 23:23; Romans 7:7; 1 Corinthians 9:8–14; James 2. Read the disputed scope in context and name interpretive frameworks when necessary.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—explain a command's stated purpose. Intermediate—apply conditions and means-based provisions. Advanced—compare related laws and prophetic priestly teaching. Master—adjudicate interacting intent, evidence, refuge and remedy conditions; distinguish historical judicial authority from private action.
- **Initial questions:** Five; dietary holiness, feast reminders, affordable offerings, priestly instruction and homicide/refuge safeguards. Further work is the largest priority: exhaustive command catalog, offerings, impurity/restoration procedures and jurisdiction-specific legal scenarios.

## Unit 07 — The Promised Land, Judges, Kings, and the Divided Kingdom

- **Objectives:** Trace entry, covenant renewal, apostasy, judges, monarchy and division; assess leaders by textual responsibilities rather than wealth or victory alone.
- **Primary passages / events:** Joshua 1–6, 23–24; Judges 2; Ruth; 1 Samuel 8–12; 2 Samuel 7, 11–12; 1 Kings 8–12; Deuteronomy 17:14–20.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Reject idolatry, preserve justice, protect the needy, constrain royal accumulation, keep and read the law; observe bounded inheritance duties (01, 09, 13, 15–17).
- **Prophetic connections:** 2 Samuel 7; Isaiah 9:6–7; Jeremiah 23:1–6. Distinguish failure of a historical king from promises of righteous government.
- **NT cross-references:** Matthew 12:3–8; Luke 1:31–33; Acts 13:16–23; Hebrews 4:1–11.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—explain Samuel's warning about royal extraction. Intermediate—trace Rehoboam's counsel and division. Advanced—compare Joshua's warning with Judges' apostasy. Master—evaluate interacting royal restrictions and confront an immunity claim using narrative rebuke and impartial justice.
- **Initial questions:** Five; warning/fulfillment, cost of kingship, heavy yoke, Solomon's obligations and Nathan's accountability lesson. Further work: conquest context, judges' cycles, Ruth's redemption and Davidic covenant distinctions.

## Unit 08 — Israel's Rebellion, Prophetic Warnings, Scattering, and Exile

- **Objectives:** Explain the prophets' identified covenant violations, distinguish Israel and Judah's historical judgments, and connect confession with responsible return.
- **Primary passages / events:** 2 Kings 17, 24–25; 2 Chronicles 36; Jeremiah 7, 25, 29; Daniel 1–9; Baruch 1:15–22; Leviticus 26; Deuteronomy 28–30. Events: Samaria's fall, Jerusalem's destruction, captivity and the Persian return opening.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Reject idols and false confidence, hear prophetic correction, protect the vulnerable, keep land responsibilities, confess and return (01, 06, 08, 11, 15, 17–18).
- **Prophetic connections:** Amos 5; Hosea 4; Ezekiel 18, 36. Teach accusations and promises in their named settings; do not reduce exile to a modern partisan analogy.
- **NT cross-references:** Matthew 21:12–13 with Jeremiah 7; Acts 7:39–43 with Amos 5; Romans 11:1–5. Baruch is used as traditional KJV Apocrypha, not a replacement for the Torah and prophetic context.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—identify a condemned action. Intermediate—explain why temple association does not excuse wrongdoing. Advanced—connect land-rest warnings with exile history. Master—compare communal confession, responsibility and restoration conditions without manufacturing a chronology or exempting leaders.
- **Initial questions:** Five; Samaria's causes, temple slogan, land sabbaths, Daniel's confession and Baruch's confession. Further work: false prophets, economic oppression, differentiated exile histories and return narratives.

## Unit 09 — Yeshua, the Apostles, the New Covenant, and Old Testament Connections

- **Objectives:** Read Yeshua's teaching, death/resurrection testimony and apostolic argument in Torah/Prophets context; distinguish a quotation, its source context and later inference.
- **Primary passages / events:** Matthew 4–7, 15, 22–28; Luke 4, 22–24; Acts 2–3, 7, 15; Romans 3–4, 9–11; Hebrews 8–10; Jeremiah 31:31–34; Ezekiel 36:22–28.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Hear and do, reject traditions that displace commandments, exercise mercy and justice, avoid boasting, turn from iniquity, understand inward law and forgiveness (01–04, 06, 08, 13, 16–18).
- **Prophetic connections:** Isaiah 53, 61; Deuteronomy 18:15–19; Genesis 22:18; Jeremiah 11, 31; Ezekiel 36. Do not claim every quotation reproduces the source word for word.
- **NT cross-references / comparison pairs:** Matthew 4 with Deuteronomy 8; Luke 4 with Isaiah 61; Acts 3 with Deuteronomy 18 and Genesis 22; Romans 11 with Jeremiah's olive imagery; Hebrews 10 with Jeremiah 31. Acts 15/Amos 9 is a future expansion requiring special attention to differing wording and debated inferences.
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—explain a teaching's direct moral demand. Intermediate—locate context traps and identify quotation boundaries. Advanced—connect promises to an apostolic explanation. Master—coordinate covenant recipients, divine agency, forgiveness and obedience without silently replacing recipients or pretending to settle all participation disputes.
- **Initial questions:** Five; wilderness word, Nazareth reading, prophet/blessing, olive warning and new-covenant comparison. Further work: resurrection witnesses, letter-specific Paul context, mercy, priesthood and bounded Gentile-council reasoning.

## Unit 10 — Israel's Restoration, Judgment, the Messiah's Reign, and the Kingdom

- **Objectives:** Explain regathering, reunited Israel, righteous government, national accountability and holy-city entry; distinguish explicit promises from speculative systems.
- **Primary passages / events:** Deuteronomy 30; Isaiah 2, 11, 60–66; Jeremiah 23, 30–33; Ezekiel 36–37; Daniel 2, 7; Micah 4; Zechariah 14; Psalms 2; Revelation 2, 19–22. Distinguish narrated return events from prophetic scenes; study event order only where the texts provide it.
- **Obligations / subject links:** Cleansing from idols, walking in statutes, righteous judgment, the nations learning Yah's ways, appointed worship and accountable leadership (01–04, 06, 12, 15–18).
- **Prophetic connections:** Two sticks/one nation, creation-order assurances, Davidic government, instruction from Zion and the rod of iron. Retain both mercy and judgment; neither cancel commandment teaching nor invent a private mandate to punish nations.
- **NT cross-references:** Luke 1:31–33; Acts 3:19–21; Romans 11:25–29; Revelation 2:26–27; 19:11–16; 21:24–27; 22:14–15 (KJV).
- **Outcomes:** Foundational—use a sign's supplied explanation. Intermediate—follow a stated worship duty and consequence. Advanced—compare covenant assurances and the source/recipients of authority. Master—integrate instruction, peace, holiness and national accountability while retaining each passage's entry conditions and refusing an unstated universal timeline.
- **Initial questions:** Five; two sticks, covenant assurance, national worship responsibility, iron-rod authority and multi-book Kingdom synthesis. Further work: righteous judicial cases, inheritance/restoration, Isaiah's nations, Daniel's Kingdom language and textual distinction between judgment scenes.

## Spiral progression proposal — not implemented

Do not lock a subject or historical unit to a single world. A possible future release could introduce all ten units in an accessible survey during Levels 1–150, revisit context and commandment conditions during 151–300, expand cross-references during 301–450, and emphasize integrated law, prophecy and leadership during 451–699. The Level 700 culmination would assess accumulated learning. These are content-planning bands, not changes to the existing seven 75-level selection stages, ten-hit combat, rank thresholds or endless progression.

A future selector should first gate on explicit approval/evidence and runtime compatibility, then retain existing hard disclosure exclusions. Score eligible questions using historical familiarity, genuinely calibrated complexity, underrepresented subjects, neglected commandment objectives, and spaced review. Preserve mixed historical units (for example, half current study emphasis, a quarter earlier review and a quarter complementary topics, when the approved pool supports it). Treat these proportions as an experiment requiring approval and validation, not new live rules.

Use concept families plus specific objectives: failure on one objective can schedule a different, related question after a cooldown; success should space later retrieval farther apart. Do not immediately reveal a missed answer through the next question. Avoid closely related concepts in one session, but retain deliberate reinforcement across sessions. Existing concept diversity is a soft preference; literal answer disclosure is a harder guard. An expanded semantic-family policy needs separate selection work and save-compatibility tests. Never pull drafts into a sparse advanced pool.

Track exposure separately from mastery: seeing one question does not complete a unit or certify a commandment. Record an objective as mastered only after spaced successful application at the appropriate complexity; the mastery rule itself is future design. The five questions per unit are an initial survey, not a course-completion assessment.

An optional future answer-reveal label could read **Lesson: Israel's Covenant at Sinai**, drawn from `editorial.courseUnitId` plus a reviewed lesson name. Do not expose editorial status or research sources as player instructions. The October 28 follow-up implements lesson titles/objectives and labels from explicit metadata. Historical selection changes remain unimplemented.
