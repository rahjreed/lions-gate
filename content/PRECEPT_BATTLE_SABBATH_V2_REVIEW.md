# Sabbath vs Sunday — Precept Chain editorial review

All ten challenges received explicit owner approval and are active in the local standalone battle. All Scripture quotations are KJV excerpts. A correct opening earns 100 points and deals 10 damage. Each correct supporting precept earns 50 points and deals 5 damage. Completing all three earns another 200 points. An opening miss costs Akobi 20 health and retries the challenge. A support miss ends the chain with earlier damage and points retained.

## Challenge set 1 — From commandment to first-day practice

### PB-SAB-01-V2 — The seventh day and the weekly rhythm

**Roman Soldier · Scripture**

> “But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God” — [Exodus 20:10](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/)

**Roman Soldier · Challenge**

Exodus names the seventh day, agreed. But if the point is a weekly rhythm modeled on creation, why couldn't another day serve the same purpose?

**Opening rebuttal**

**A · Proposed strongest**. A rhythm tells you how often. It doesn't tell you the date. Genesis sets the day apart, and Exodus carries that same day into the command.

*Feedback.* Genesis blesses a particular day. Exodus carries that specificity into the command.

**B**. Creation gives us a pattern of work and rest. I'd keep that pattern and let each congregation choose the day that serves it best.

*Feedback.* The rest explains the rhythm, but the next verse blesses a named day. The excerpt supplies no basis for leaving the day open.

**Precept 1**

Back it up with Scripture!

**A**. “he rested on the seventh day from all his work” — [Genesis 2:2](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Genesis-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* The rest explains the rhythm, but the next verse blesses a named day. The excerpt supplies no basis for leaving the day open. It is weaker evidence for the opening rebuttal.

**B · Proposed strongest**. “And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it” — [Genesis 2:3](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Genesis-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* Genesis blesses a particular day. Exodus carries that specificity into the command.

**Precept 2**

Bring another witness!

**A**. “Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.” — [Exodus 20:8](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/)

*Feedback.* This gives the command to keep the Sabbath holy. The excerpt does not explain the creation basis that answers the claim about choosing another day.

**B · Proposed strongest**. “wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it” — [Exodus 20:11](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/)

*Feedback.* This links the command to the day God rested and blessed. It explains why the creation pattern is tied to that specific day.

**Editorial review.** Distinguish the day named in the command from debates about later covenant application. Review whether the first excerpt directly supports the opening and whether the second adds a distinct point rather than merely repeating it.

*Feedback.* The Lord’s authority is explicit, but the disciples are never shown receiving authority to move the day. The dispute is over their conduct on it.

**B · Proposed strongest**. David's hunger explains why Yeshua answered the way he did. They were arguing over what the disciples did that day. Who asked him to move it?

**Precept 1**

What precept proves your point?

**A · Proposed strongest**. “when he had need, and was an hungred” — [Mark 2:25](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

**B**. “Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.” — [Mark 2:28](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* His authority is explicit. The passage never says he delegated authority to change the day to his disciples. It is weaker evidence for the opening rebuttal.

**Precept 2**

Can you establish that with another witness?

**A · Proposed strongest**. “why do they on the sabbath day that which is not lawful?” — [Mark 2:24](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

**B**. “Is it lawful to do good on the sabbath days, or to do evil?” — [Mark 3:4](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-3/)

*Feedback.* This is a strong parallel about what can be done on the Sabbath. The charge in the grainfield scene is the more direct evidence for the specific dispute Akobi is answering.

**Editorial review.** Readers differ on the wider scope of his authority here. Grade the action and conclusion shown in this scene. Review whether the first excerpt directly supports the opening and whether the second adds a distinct point rather than merely repeating it.

*Feedback.* Luke connects the earlier rest to the commandment. The first-day visit is a separate event.

**Precept 2**

*Feedback.* The empty tomb is central to the event, but this line gives no sequence of days and no instruction about weekly rest.

**Editorial review.** The passage dates the visit. Do not infer an exact resurrection hour or dismiss first-day commemoration. Review whether the first excerpt directly supports the opening and whether the second adds a distinct point rather than merely repeating it.

## Challenge set 2 — The Lord's day, judgment, and rest

### PB-SAB-06-V2 — The Lord’s day

**Roman Soldier · Scripture**

> “I was in the Spirit on the Lord’s day” — [Revelation 1:10](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Revelation-Chapter-1/)

**Roman Soldier · Challenge**

The churches probably knew what John meant by the Lord's day. With the resurrection discovered on the first day, why not take John's title as Sunday?

**Opening rebuttal**

**A**. Yeshua calls himself Lord of the Sabbath. That connection makes sense to me, so I'd identify the day John names with the seventh day.

*Feedback.* Mark establishes Yeshua’s lordship over the Sabbath. That does not by itself define the title John uses in Revelation.

**B · Proposed strongest**. Luke gives the tomb visit a weekday. John gives his day a title. Show me where Scripture connects the two before I call them the same day.

*Feedback.* Luke dates the visit; Revelation uses a title without naming a weekday. Linking those passages is an inference, just as linking John’s phrase to Sabbath lordship is an inference.

**Precept 1**

Let’s test the link I’m making. What’s your witness?

**A**. “Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.” — [Mark 2:28](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* This makes a Sabbath association plausible. It addresses Yeshua’s authority and does not test whether Luke’s first-day account supplies the link the Roman claims.

**B · Proposed strongest**. “Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre” — [Luke 24:1](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Luke-Chapter-24/)

*Feedback.* This is the evidence behind the Roman’s inference. Luke dates the tomb visit but does not use John’s title or connect that title to the visit.

**Precept 2**

Can you show me the difference between a date and a title?

**A · Proposed strongest**. “the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God” — [Exodus 20:10](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/)

*Feedback.* Exodus expressly connects a weekday to a sacred designation. That gives a useful contrast with Revelation 1:10, without proving John meant the Sabbath.

**B**. “And upon the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread” — [Acts 20:7](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Acts-Chapter-20/)

*Feedback.* This supports first-day gathering as a biblical practice. Like Luke’s date, it leaves John’s title undefined and supplies no explicit assignment of that title.

**Editorial review.** Sunday, seventh-day Sabbath, and prophetic-day readings of Revelation 1:10 remain disputed. The first precept tests the Roman’s link to the tomb visit. The second asks what an explicit assignment of a weekday looks like, not which day John meant.

### PB-SAB-08-V2 — Shadows and judgment

**Roman Soldier · Scripture**

> “Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days” — [Colossians 2:16](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

**Roman Soldier · Challenge**

Colossians actually names sabbath days and calls the observances shadows. If they point to Christ, why would Paul still expect people to observe them?

**Opening rebuttal**

**A**. If the shadow points to Christ, I can see why Paul wouldn't judge people over keeping it. I'd take that to mean its obligation has run its course.

*Feedback.* The shadow language can inform a reading about obligation. It does not by itself turn Paul’s instruction about judgment into a command to stop observing the Sabbath.

**B · Proposed strongest**. A shadow can point you somewhere without being forbidden. Paul tells them how to treat each other over these observances. Where does he tell them to stop?

*Feedback.* Paul's direct instruction forbids judgment over the listed matters. A ban on observance requires a further argument.

**Precept 1**

What else in the argument helps you tell those apart?

**A · Proposed strongest**. “after the commandments and doctrines of men?” — [Colossians 2:22](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* Read with verses 20–23, this identifies the source of the regulations Paul challenges, including the restrictions named in verse 21. It gives context for resisting imposed rules without treating the Sabbath itself as a human invention.

**B**. “Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.” — [Colossians 2:17](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* This explains the Roman’s appeal to Christ. The metaphor does not identify the imposed rules discussed later or tell the reader to stop observing the Sabbath.

**Precept 2**

Where else does Paul make that distinction in practice?

**A · Proposed strongest**. “Let not him that eateth despise him that eateth not; and let not him which eateth not judge him that eateth: for God hath received him.” — [Romans 14:3](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Romans-Chapter-14/)

*Feedback.* Paul warns both sides in a dispute about eating against condemning one another. It illustrates how a judgment instruction can address treatment of people without forbidding the practice being debated.

**B**. “He that regardeth the day, regardeth it unto the Lord; and he that regardeth not the day, to the Lord he doth not regard it.” — [Romans 14:6](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Romans-Chapter-14/)

*Feedback.* This addresses differing ways of regarding an unnamed day. It is relevant to liberty of conscience, but does not directly show the distinction between judging people and prohibiting their practice.

**Editorial review.** Readers disagree about the scope of sabbath days, the implications of the shadow image, and the relationship of verses 20–23 to verse 16. Do not equate God’s Sabbath with human regulations or present Romans 14 as naming the weekly Sabbath. Grade the distinction between a stated instruction about judgment and a further conclusion about obligation.
