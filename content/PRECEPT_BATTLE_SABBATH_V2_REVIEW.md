# Sabbath vs Sunday — V2 editorial review

Pending approval. KJV quotations below are excerpts.

## Chain 1 — From commandment to first-day practice

### PB-SAB-01-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God” — [Exodus 20:10](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/)

I grant Exodus names the seventh day. But isn't the lasting principle one day in seven, with the day itself adaptable?

**A**. The creation account emphasizes God's rest, so I'd carry over the rhythm and leave the choice of day open.

“he rested on the seventh day from all his work” — [Genesis 2:2](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Genesis-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* The rest explains the rhythm, but the next verse blesses a named day. The excerpt supplies no basis for leaving the day open.

**B · Proposed strongest**. The rhythm matters, but Genesis blesses the seventh day itself. Exodus keeps that same day in the command.

“And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it” — [Genesis 2:3](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Genesis-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* Genesis blesses a particular day. Exodus carries that specificity into the command.

**Editorial review.** Distinguish the day named in the command from debates about later covenant application.

### PB-SAB-02-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath” — [Mark 2:27](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

Fair. But if the Sabbath serves people, why couldn't Yeshua's disciples adapt the day to their needs?

**A · Proposed strongest**. Yeshua brings up David's hunger because the dispute is over what they did that day, not which day to keep.

“when he had need, and was an hungred” — [Mark 2:25](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* David's need answers the charge over the disciples' conduct on the Sabbath already in view.

**B**. If Yeshua is Lord of the Sabbath, his disciples can adapt the day when their mission calls for it.

“Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.” — [Mark 2:28](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* His authority is explicit. The passage never says he delegated authority to change the day to his disciples.

**Editorial review.** Readers differ on the wider scope of his authority here. Grade the action and conclusion shown in this scene.

### PB-SAB-03-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre” — [Luke 24:1](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Luke-Chapter-24/)

Then let's look beyond the grainfields. The first-day visit marks the discovery of the resurrection. Isn't that reason to make it the new Sabbath?

**A · Proposed strongest**. The empty tomb gives us a reason to celebrate, but Luke puts the commanded rest before that first-day visit.

“rested the sabbath day according to the commandment.” — [Luke 23:56](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Luke-Chapter-23/)

*Feedback.* Luke connects the earlier rest to the commandment. The first-day visit is a separate event.

**B**. The resurrection is central enough that the day the women heard the news should become the church's weekly rest day.

“He is not here, but is risen” — [Luke 24:6](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Luke-Chapter-24/)

*Feedback.* The announcement supports celebration. It gives no weekly rest instruction and does not say the earlier Sabbath was replaced.

**Editorial review.** The passage dates the visit. Do not infer an exact resurrection hour or dismiss first-day commemoration.

### PB-SAB-04-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “And upon the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread, Paul preached unto them” — [Acts 20:7](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Acts-Chapter-20/)

All right, the tomb account records an event. At Troas, though, the disciples actually gather and break bread. Why isn't that a weekly pattern?

**A**. Breaking bread was already a regular practice, so the first-day meeting at Troas gives us their normal weekly schedule.

“fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.” — [Acts 2:42](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Acts-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* Acts 2 supports regular fellowship and breaking bread without naming a weekday. One dated meeting does not establish a schedule.

**B · Proposed strongest**. They really did meet that day, but Paul was leaving the next day. Luke gives no rule for future gatherings.

“Paul preached unto them, ready to depart on the morrow” — [Acts 20:7](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Acts-Chapter-20/)

*Feedback.* Paul's departure gives the meeting its setting. Luke reports this gathering without prescribing a weekly schedule.

**Editorial review.** The nature of breaking bread and ancient day boundaries need not be settled to answer this claim.

### PB-SAB-05-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “Upon the first day of the week let every one of you lay by him in store” — [1 Corinthians 16:2](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/1-Corinthians-Chapter-16/)

A meeting could be incidental. Paul gave Corinth and Galatia a first-day instruction, though. Doesn't that show a settled weekly practice?

**A**. Paul tells everyone to set something aside on the first day, so the gifts point to a regular church gathering.

“let every one of you lay by him in store” — [1 Corinthians 16:2](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/1-Corinthians-Chapter-16/)

*Feedback.* The verse says what each person should do and when. It does not give a meeting as the setting.

**B · Proposed strongest**. Corinth and Galatia got the same instruction about gifts for the saints. That tells us what the schedule was for.

“Now concerning the collection for the saints” — [1 Corinthians 16:1](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/1-Corinthians-Chapter-16/)

*Feedback.* Paul identifies the subject before specifying the day. The schedule concerns the collection.

**Editorial review.** Do not treat the location of the set-aside as settled by the wording.

## Chain 2 — The Lord's day, judgment, and rest

### PB-SAB-06-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “I was in the Spirit on the Lord’s day” — [Revelation 1:10](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Revelation-Chapter-1/)

I'll grant the collection has its own purpose. But John uses a title his readers would recognize. Why wouldn't the Lord's day mean Sunday?

**A · Proposed strongest**. John locates his vision on the Lord's day. He gives the day a title but never names its weekday.

“I was in the Spirit on the Lord’s day” — [Revelation 1:10](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Revelation-Chapter-1/)

*Feedback.* John gives a title and describes the vision. The line supplies no weekday or weekly rest command.

**B**. Yeshua calls himself Lord of the Sabbath, so I'd take John's title as a definite reference to the seventh day.

“Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.” — [Mark 2:28](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* Mark establishes Yeshua's authority over Sabbath. It does not define John's phrase in a different passage.

**Editorial review.** Editorial review should ensure the key addresses what the text establishes, rather than grading a particular historical identification.

### PB-SAB-07-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “One man esteemeth one day above another: another esteemeth every day alike.” — [Romans 14:5](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Romans-Chapter-14/)

Even if John doesn't settle the weekday, Paul lets believers regard days differently. Why would Sabbath be exempt from that freedom?

**A**. Paul tells believers to stop judging each other, so I would treat every weekly observance as a personal choice.

“Let us not therefore judge one another any more” — [Romans 14:13](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Romans-Chapter-14/)

*Feedback.* The instruction concerns how believers treat one another. It does not by itself identify every weekly observance as optional.

**B · Proposed strongest**. Paul puts different ways of regarding days alongside eating. He leaves the day unnamed, so the Sabbath application needs another step.

“He that regardeth the day, regardeth it unto the Lord” — [Romans 14:6](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Romans-Chapter-14/)

*Feedback.* Paul describes honoring the day alongside eating unto the Lord. The statement names no specific observance.

**Editorial review.** The range of days in Romans 14 is disputed. The key should remain a claim about the text's scope.

### PB-SAB-08-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days” — [Colossians 2:16](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

Romans doesn't name it, but Colossians does. If Sabbath belongs with shadows fulfilled in Christ, why keep it as an obligation?

**A · Proposed strongest**. Paul calls these observances shadows, and his instruction is about judgment. He doesn't tell anyone to stop observing the Sabbath.

“Let no man therefore judge you” — [Colossians 2:16](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* Paul's direct instruction forbids judgment over the listed matters. A ban on observance requires a further argument.

**B**. Since the shadow points to Christ, I'd take keeping the weekly Sabbath as an obligation Paul expects believers to leave behind.

“Which are a shadow of things to come” — [Colossians 2:17](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* The image points to Christ, but it does not expressly forbid the practice. Paul's stated instruction addresses judgment.

**Editorial review.** Review weekly versus festival scope and the force of the shadow image. The answer need not settle either.

### PB-SAB-09-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God.” — [Hebrews 4:9](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Hebrews-Chapter-4/)

Granted, Colossians isn't a ban. But Hebrews says a rest remains for believers. Isn't that the rest the weekly Sabbath was pointing toward?

**A · Proposed strongest**. Hebrews brings God's seventh-day rest into the argument about entering his rest. It never says that entering it cancels the weekly day.

“And God did rest the seventh day from all his works.” — [Hebrews 4:4](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Hebrews-Chapter-4/)

*Feedback.* The creation reference links the themes. Hebrews urges entry into the promised rest without giving a weekly calendar change.

**B**. Hebrews urges believers to enter God's rest, so that promise takes over the role the weekly Sabbath used to serve.

“Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest” — [Hebrews 4:11](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Hebrews-Chapter-4/)

*Feedback.* The exhortation concerns entering the promised rest. It does not say the promise replaces weekly observance.

**Editorial review.** The meaning of the remaining rest is debated. The strongest option allows a wider meaning while answering the implied replacement.

### PB-SAB-10-V2

**Roman Soldier**

> “And upon the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread” — [Acts 20:7](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Acts-Chapter-20/)

Put Troas together with Colossians. A first-day gathering and freedom from judgment over Sabbaths seem like grounds for calling Sunday the new Sabbath.

**A**. The disciples gathered on the first day, and Paul warned against judgment. That gives churches room to call Sunday their Sabbath.

“in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days” — [Colossians 2:16](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

*Feedback.* A precedent for gathering and a warning against judgment may inform practice. Neither makes the gathering day take over the commandment.

**B · Proposed strongest**. Troas shows a meeting, and Colossians addresses judgment. Neither changes the seventh day Exodus actually names as the Sabbath.

“the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God” — [Exodus 20:10](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/)

*Feedback.* Exodus names the day. A meeting and a warning against judgment do not together state a transfer of Sabbath rest.

**Editorial review.** Review whether the alternative clearly claims authority to call Sunday the Sabbath. A narrower claim about permission to gather on Sunday would be defensible.
