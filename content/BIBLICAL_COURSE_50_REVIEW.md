## Question index

### Unit 01 — Creation, the Fall, and the Beginning

- **course-creation-sanctified-rest-reason** — editorial 2; complexity 1; subject 02. What reason does Genesis give for God blessing and sanctifying the seventh day?
  Proposed answer: He rested in it from his completed work of creation.
  References: Genesis 2:1-3.
- **course-eden-expulsion-tree-access** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject general. Why is the way to the tree of life guarded after the man is sent out of Eden?
  Proposed answer: To prevent him from taking its fruit and living for ever.
  References: Genesis 3:22-24.
- **course-cain-accepted-doing-well** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject 06. When Cain is angry about his offering, what does God’s reply in Genesis 4:6–7 direct attention toward?
  Proposed answer: Doing well and the danger of sin at the door.
  References: Genesis 4:3-7.
- **course-image-and-shared-dominion** — editorial 2; complexity 1; subject general. How does Genesis 1:26–28 connect humanity’s creation with its assigned role?
  Proposed answer: Male and female bear God’s image and receive dominion over living creatures.
  References: Genesis 1:26-28.
- **course-adam-christ-obedience-contrast** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject 01. Comparing Genesis 3:17–19 with Romans 5:15–19, which account preserves Paul’s contrast between Adam and Christ?
  Proposed answer: Adam’s offence brings condemnation; Christ’s obedience brings righteousness and life.
  References: Romans 5:15-19; Genesis 3:17-19.

### Unit 02 — Noah, Babel, and the Nations

- **course-flood-corruption-violence-cause** — editorial 2; complexity 1; subject general. Which condition does God expressly give Noah as a reason for the approaching destruction in Genesis 6:13?
  Proposed answer: The earth is filled with violence through its inhabitants.
  References: Genesis 6:5-13.
- **course-noah-promise-and-blood-duty** — editorial 7; complexity 5; subject 01. How should Noah’s post-flood assurances and instructions be distinguished across Genesis 8:20–22 and 9:3–17?
  Proposed answer: Seasonal continuity and no world-destroying flood accompany restrictions concerning blood and human life.
  References: Genesis 9:8-17; Genesis 8:20-22; Genesis 9:3-6.
- **course-human-life-image-ground** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject general. What reason does Genesis 9:6 give for the accountability attached to shedding human blood?
  Proposed answer: God made man in his own image.
  References: Genesis 9:4-6.
- **course-babel-plan-and-scattering** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject general. How does the outcome at Babel reverse the builders’ stated aim?
  Proposed answer: They seek to avoid scattering, but God scatters them and their building stops.
  References: Genesis 11:1-9.
- **course-nations-origin-seeking-accountability** — editorial 7; complexity 5; subject 18. Which synthesis respects Genesis 10–11 and Paul’s address in Acts 17 without identifying any modern people with an ancient genealogy?
  Proposed answer: Genesis traces nations and scattering; Paul affirms their dependence on the Creator and calls all to repent before judgment.
  References: Acts 17:24-31; Genesis 10:32; Genesis 11:8-9.

### Unit 03 — Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and the Covenant

- **course-patriarch-promise-continuity** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject 01. Which thread continues as the promises to Abraham are spoken to Isaac and Jacob?
  Proposed answer: Promised blessing and land continue through the family, with blessing extending to other families or nations.
  References: Genesis 12:1-3; Genesis 26:3-5; Genesis 28:13-15.
- **course-amorite-iniquity-return-timing** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 18. What reason accompanies the descendants’ delayed return in Genesis 15:16?
  Proposed answer: The iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.
  References: Genesis 15:13-16.
- **course-abraham-sign-household-scope** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 01. Which description preserves the scope of the circumcision instruction to Abraham’s household in Genesis 17?
  Proposed answer: It includes male children born in the house and those bought from a stranger.
  References: Genesis 17:9-14.
- **course-abraham-household-justice-purpose** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject 16. What household responsibility accompanies Abraham’s promised national future in Genesis 18:19?
  Proposed answer: Teaching his household to keep the LORD’s way and do justice and judgment.
  References: Genesis 18:17-19.
- **course-patriarch-inheritance-promise-possession** — editorial 8; complexity 6; subject 17. An interpreter says Abraham’s sojourning proves the inheritance promise failed. Which reading best accounts for Genesis 23:4, Acts 7:5 and Hebrews 11:8–16 together?
  Proposed answer: Distinguish the promised inheritance from Abraham’s sojourning; Hebrews describes continued faith and the country and city sought.
  References: Hebrews 11:8-16; Genesis 23:4; Acts 7:5.

### Unit 04 — The Twelve Tribes, Joseph, and Egyptian Captivity

- **course-judah-sale-and-benjamin-substitution** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject general. What contrast appears between Judah’s proposal about Joseph in Genesis 37 and his appeal for Benjamin in Genesis 44?
  Proposed answer: He proposes selling Joseph, but later offers to remain a servant in Benjamin’s place out of concern for his father.
  References: Genesis 37:26-28; Genesis 44:30-34.
- **course-joseph-surplus-famine-policy** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 16. Why does Joseph recommend gathering food during the plentiful years?
  Proposed answer: To store supplies against the coming famine so the land does not perish.
  References: Genesis 41:33-36.
- **course-ephraim-manasseh-inheritance-adoption** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 09. What inheritance distinction does Jacob make concerning Joseph’s sons in Genesis 48:5–6?
  Proposed answer: Ephraim and Manasseh count as his own sons; later offspring are named under their brothers in inheritance.
  References: Genesis 48:5-6.
- **course-egypt-oppression-not-promise-ended** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject 01. How should Joseph’s deathbed expectation be read alongside the oppression under Egypt’s new king?
  Proposed answer: Joseph expects God to bring Israel to the sworn land; the later oppression describes their condition before that deliverance.
  References: Exodus 1:8-14; Genesis 50:24-25.
- **course-joseph-providence-not-excusing-evil** — editorial 8; complexity 6; subject 16. A leader says Joseph’s rise proves betrayal is righteous when it produces prosperity. Which reading preserves Genesis 50, Acts 7 and Psalm 105 together?
  Proposed answer: The brothers’ intent remains evil and envious; God’s preserving purpose and Joseph’s care do not relabel that intent as righteous.
  References: Genesis 50:19-21; Acts 7:9-10; Psalms 105:17-24.

### Unit 05 — Moses, the Exodus, Sinai, and the Giving of the Law

- **course-exodus-redemption-people-inheritance** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject 01. Which purpose accompanies the promised release from Egyptian bondage in Exodus 6:6–8?
  Proposed answer: God takes Israel as his people and brings them toward the land sworn to the patriarchs.
  References: Exodus 6:6-8.
- **course-passover-children-memorial-meaning** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject 03. When children ask the meaning of the Passover service, what explanation are Israelite households instructed to give?
  Proposed answer: The LORD passed over their houses when he struck Egypt and delivered their households.
  References: Exodus 12:24-27.
- **course-manna-sabbath-preparation-pattern** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 02. How does the manna instruction in Exodus 16 provide for seventh-day rest?
  Proposed answer: A double portion is gathered on the sixth day and kept for the day when none is found in the field.
  References: Exodus 16:22-30.
- **course-sinai-treasure-condition** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject 01. What condition is attached to Israel being a peculiar treasure in the Sinai address?
  Proposed answer: Obeying God’s voice and keeping his covenant.
  References: Exodus 19:4-6.
- **course-sinai-words-assent-blood-comparison** — editorial 7; complexity 5; subject 01. Which account keeps distinct the roles of words, mediator, people and blood in Exodus 24, Deuteronomy 5 and Hebrews 9?
  Proposed answer: Moses mediates the words, the people assent to obedience, and blood marks the covenant’s dedication.
  References: Exodus 24:3-8; Deuteronomy 5:2-5; Hebrews 9:18-22.

### Unit 06 — The Torah, Commandments, Holiness, and Covenant Responsibilities

- **course-holiness-dietary-separation-purpose** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject 04. How are clean-and-unclean distinctions related to Israel’s calling in Leviticus 20:24–26?
  Proposed answer: Israel must distinguish them as a people separated to belong to the holy LORD.
  References: Leviticus 20:24-26.
- **course-weeks-booths-memory-distinction** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject 03. Which comparison preserves the different historical reminders attached to Weeks and dwelling in booths?
  Proposed answer: Weeks recalls bondage in Egypt; booths teach later generations about Israel’s wilderness dwelling.
  References: Deuteronomy 16:9-12; Leviticus 23:39-43.
- **course-poor-offerer-means-and-flour** — editorial 5; complexity 3; subject 12. For the sins addressed in Leviticus 5:7–13, how does the required offering change when the person cannot afford the birds?
  Proposed answer: Fine flour is allowed, without oil or frankincense, and the priest makes atonement.
  References: Leviticus 5:7-13.
- **course-priest-teaching-distinction-continuity** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject 12. Which responsibility is shared by Aaron’s instruction in Leviticus 10 and the priestly description in Ezekiel 44:23?
  Proposed answer: Teaching the people to distinguish holy from profane and clean from unclean.
  References: Leviticus 10:8-11; Ezekiel 44:23.
- **course-homicide-refuge-ransom-judicial-synthesis** — editorial 10; complexity 7; subject 13. In Torah’s judicial setting, an axe-head kills a neighbor without prior hatred; a second man plots a killing and seeks the altar. A lone witness and a payment offer are then proposed. Which ruling preserves all four cited safeguards?
  Proposed answer: Judge the accidental slayer’s refuge claim; the altar does not shelter guile; one witness cannot establish a capital charge, and ransom cannot replace a murderer’s sentence.
  References: Numbers 35:22-25; Numbers 35:30-34; Deuteronomy 19:4-6; Exodus 21:12-14.

### Unit 07 — The Promised Land, Judges, Kings, and the Divided Kingdom

- **course-joshua-warning-judges-forsaking** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject 15. Which development in Judges 2 exemplifies the danger addressed in Joshua’s farewell warning?
  Proposed answer: A later generation forsakes the LORD and serves the surrounding peoples’ gods.
  References: Joshua 23:6-13; Judges 2:10-13.
- **course-samuel-kingship-cost-extraction** — editorial 3; complexity 2; subject 16. What practical danger dominates Samuel’s description of the king Israel requests?
  Proposed answer: The king will take people, produce and property for his own service.
  References: 1 Samuel 8:11-18.
- **course-rehoboam-yoke-counsel-consequence** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 16. What choice by Rehoboam immediately precedes the northern tribes’ rejection of his rule in 1 Kings 12?
  Proposed answer: He rejects advice to serve the people and promises a heavier yoke.
  References: 1 Kings 12:6-16.
- **course-solomon-royal-prohibitions-history** — editorial 8; complexity 6; subject 16. A counselor argues that royal prosperity excuses Solomon’s acquisitions. Which assessment best preserves Deuteronomy 17 and 1 Kings 10–11?
  Proposed answer: Evaluate Egyptian horses, multiplied wealth and wives against the royal limits; Kings also records his heart turning after other gods.
  References: Deuteronomy 17:16-17; 1 Kings 10:26-29; 1 Kings 11:1-10.
- **course-nathan-royal-accountability-justice** — editorial 7; complexity 5; subject 16. A ruler claims his office makes correction improper. Which response brings Nathan’s confrontation, Leviticus 19:15 and Proverbs 31:8–9 together?
  Proposed answer: Judge without favor to the mighty, defend the needy, and recognize that Nathan charges the king with despising God’s commandment.
  References: 2 Samuel 12:1-9; Leviticus 19:15; Proverbs 31:8-9.

- **course-samaria-exile-prophets-rejected** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 15. How does 2 Kings 17 explain Israel’s removal beyond the military event itself?
  Proposed answer: Israel rejects the covenant, serves other gods and refuses prophetic calls to return.
  References: 2 Kings 17:7-18.
- **course-temple-slogan-not-moral-immunity** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 06. Why does Jeremiah challenge confidence in the repeated cry, “The temple of the LORD”?

  References: Jeremiah 7:3-11.
- **course-exile-land-sabbaths-covenant-warning** — editorial 6; complexity 4; subject 11. How does Chronicles’ account of the desolate land connect with Leviticus 26’s warning?
  Proposed answer: The exile leaves the land desolate to enjoy the sabbaths it had lacked while inhabited.
  References: 2 Chronicles 36:14-21; Leviticus 26:33-35.
- **course-daniel-confession-return-covenant** — editorial 7; complexity 5; subject 01. Which comparison best connects Daniel’s exile confession with Deuteronomy 30’s return passage?

  References: Daniel 9:4-14; Deuteronomy 30:1-3.
- **course-baruch-confession-commandments-prophets** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 01. What explanation does Baruch’s confession give for the calamities cleaving to Judah and Jerusalem?

  References: Baruch 1:15-22.

- **course-two-sticks-one-people-obedience** — editorial 4; complexity 3; subject 17. What does the joining of the two sticks signify in Ezekiel’s own explanation?

  References: Ezekiel 37:15-24.

  References: Jeremiah 31:35-37; Jeremiah 33:20-26.
