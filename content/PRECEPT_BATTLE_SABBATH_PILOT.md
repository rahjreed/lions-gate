# Lion's Gate — Sabbath vs Sunday

Pilot `pb-sabbath-01`, revision 1. Draft for editorial review. The Roman presents arguments some Christians make, and the player selects the reply best supported by the cited KJV text. The debate tests what those passages establish about the seventh-day Sabbath and a proposed first-day replacement.

## Chain 1 — From commandment to first-day practice

### PB-SAB-01 · Which day?

**Roman:** “Surely the command is to give God one day in seven. Why insist on the seventh?”

- A. The command sets a six-to-one pattern and leaves the choice of day to each household.
- B. The command identifies the seventh day and ties its blessing to God's rest at creation.
- C. The command measures rest from the end of each worker's own six shifts.
- D. The command names the day for Israel's leaders and leaves households to set their own.

**Key B.** The command names the seventh day, extends rest to those within the gates, and grounds it in creation. [Exodus 20:8–11](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/)

### PB-SAB-02 · The grainfields

**Roman:** “Exodus names the seventh. But when the disciples plucked grain, didn't Yeshua treat Sabbath observance as optional?”

- A. He explains that the disciples were walking before the Sabbath began.
- B. He grants the disciples a permanent exemption because they are following him.
- C. He condemns the disciples' action but pardons it because of their hunger.
- D. He answers the accusation with David's need, the Sabbath's purpose, and his authority over it.

**Key D.** Yeshua uses David's need to answer the challenge over plucking grain. His reply addresses that dispute and names no new day. [Mark 2:23–28](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Mark-Chapter-2/)

### PB-SAB-03 · The empty tomb

**Roman:** “Granted, the grainfield dispute isn't a calendar change. But surely the empty tomb on the first day makes that day the new Sabbath?”

- A. Luke distinguishes the commanded Sabbath rest from the first-day visit to the tomb.
- B. Luke reports that the women rested on the first day after finding the tomb empty.
- C. Luke describes the first-day visit as the act by which the women kept the Sabbath command.
- D. Luke reports that the women brought the spices before taking their Sabbath rest.

**Key A.** The women prepared spices, rested according to the commandment, then found the tomb empty on their first-day visit. The account distinguishes the days without stating the hour of resurrection or transferring the Sabbath. [Luke 23:54–56](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Luke-Chapter-23/) · [Luke 24:1–3](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Luke-Chapter-24/)

### PB-SAB-04 · Troas

**Roman:** “Luke gives no transfer command at the tomb. But the disciples actually broke bread on the first day at Troas. Isn't that the apostolic precedent?”

- A. The account identifies the meeting as an observance of the new weekly Sabbath.
- B. Paul tells the disciples to repeat the gathering every first day after he leaves.
- C. Acts records a first-day gathering and preaching before Paul's departure, without prescribing a new rest day.
- D. The meeting is said to mark the weekly anniversary of the resurrection.

**Key C.** The gathering and breaking of bread are real. The account describes Paul's extended address before departure, without prescribing a weekly rest day. [Acts 20:6–12](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Acts-Chapter-20/)

### PB-SAB-05 · The collection

**Roman:** “Troas may be one meeting. But Paul gave more than one church a first-day instruction. Doesn't that make the practice binding?”

- A. Paul directs the churches to receive the gifts during a first-day breaking of bread.
- B. Paul tells each person to set aside a gift so the collection is ready when he arrives.
- C. Paul tells the churches to bring the gifts each first day to the delegates bound for Jerusalem.
- D. Paul designates the first day as a day of rest so that everyone can prepare their gift.

**Key B.** The instruction applies to a collection for the saints. Paul specifies setting aside gifts before his arrival and names Jerusalem as the destination. He gives no order here for a worship assembly or Sabbath rest. [1 Corinthians 16:1–4](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/1-Corinthians-Chapter-16/)

## Chain 2 — The Lord's day, judgment, and rest

### PB-SAB-06 · John's vision

**Roman:** “Very well, the collection proves a collection. But John calls it the Lord's day. Doesn't that give the first day its sacred title?”

- A. John defines the expression by calling it the seventh day of creation.
- B. John identifies it by recalling the disciples' first-day meeting at Troas.
- C. John uses the phrase for his experience in the Spirit and gives no weekday in the passage.
- D. John identifies the day through the churches' observance of the resurrection.

**Key C.** John places the vision on the Lord's day. The passage itself gives no weekday or instruction to change the Sabbath. Identifying the phrase with Sunday depends on an argument beyond the verse. [Revelation 1:9–11](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Revelation-Chapter-1/)

### PB-SAB-07 · Days and conscience

**Roman:** “Even if John leaves the weekday unnamed, Paul allows one believer to esteem a day and another to esteem all alike. Doesn't that settle the Sabbath question?”

- A. Paul addresses differing practices and judgment among believers, without naming the Sabbath in this discussion.
- B. Paul tells believers who esteem different days to adopt the same day for the sake of unity.
- C. Paul specifically names the seventh day as one of the days each believer may set aside.
- D. Paul identifies the days as fasts and directs both groups to keep them alike.

**Key A.** The discussion puts days and eating alongside the call to receive one another. The scope of its application is debated, and the passage does not identify the Sabbath or name a replacement. [Romans 14:1–6, 10–13](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Romans-Chapter-14/)

### PB-SAB-08 · Shadows and judgment

**Roman:** “Romans leaves the day unnamed, but Colossians actually mentions sabbath days and calls them shadows. Doesn't that make the seventh-day command obsolete?”

- A. Paul names the weekly Sabbath as the one observance excluded from his list.
- B. Paul forbids judgment because the assemblies have already changed to the first day.
- C. Paul assigns the listed observances to people outside Christ and forbids believers to observe them.
- D. Paul forbids judgment on the listed matters and uses shadow language, without stating a first-day replacement.

**Key D.** Paul names sabbath days among the matters on which believers should not be judged and relates the shadows to Christ. The passage does not identify Sunday or issue a new weekly command. The wider implications of the shadow language are interpretive. [Colossians 2:13–17](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

### PB-SAB-09 · A rest that remains

**Roman:** “I grant that Paul doesn't name a replacement. But Hebrews speaks of a rest believers enter. Doesn't that rest take the place of a weekly Sabbath?”

- A. Hebrews says the rest was fully given when Israel first entered the land.
- B. Hebrews links entry into God's rest with God's seventh-day rest, without saying one cancels the other.
- C. Hebrews identifies the remaining rest as a new first-day practice for believers.
- D. Hebrews discusses rest only as the weekly cessation of work, apart from faith or unbelief.

**Key B.** Hebrews cites God's seventh-day rest, warns about unbelief, and urges entry into the rest that remains. The chapter does not issue a calendar change or explicitly revoke the weekly Sabbath. [Hebrews 4:1–11](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Hebrews-Chapter-4/)

### PB-SAB-10 · The combined case

**Roman:** “One last challenge. Put the first-day meeting beside Paul's warning against judging over sabbath days. Taken together, why shouldn't I call Sunday the new Sabbath?”

- A. Since neither passage commands a transfer, neither permits believers to gather on the first day.
- B. The meeting establishes a new weekly Sabbath, and Paul's warning shows why no command was needed.
- C. The texts show a first-day meeting and a warning against judgment. Neither changes the day Exodus names.
- D. Paul's warning requires every church to choose its own replacement for the seventh day.

**Key C.** Exodus designates the seventh day. Acts describes a first-day gathering, and Colossians addresses judgment about observances. The combined case still requires an inference to reach a Sunday Sabbath. [Exodus 20:8–11](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Exodus-Chapter-20/) · [Acts 20:6–12](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Acts-Chapter-20/) · [Colossians 2:16–17](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/Colossians-Chapter-2/)

**Closing line after the final strike:** “I showed thee a meeting and a warning. I did not show thee a command to change the day.”
